# The update shape

Use this when you write an update by hand, or when you want to check a draft.

## The three questions

A client reads an update to find out three things.

1. Is my thing moving?
2. Is anything waiting on me?
3. Is anything wrong?

If your update answers those, it is a good update.

## The shape

Three or four short paragraphs. No bullet points. It reads like you typed it on your phone.

```
Hey {first name}, quick one.

{What got finished. One sentence per thing. Say what is now true for them.}

{What is moving now. If it is still being tested, say so.}

{The one thing you need from them, in a sentence. If you need nothing, leave this out.}
```

## Where the facts come from

Write from the record. Do not write from memory.

- The board. What got finished, what is in progress.
- The code. What shipped.
- The channel. What they already know, and what they asked.
- The calls. What was promised.
- Their accounts. The numbers.
- The end of day log. What nobody else wrote down.

You do not need all six. Start with the ones you have.

## The read, before you send

Cut these:

- What they already know.
- How it works under the hood.
- A scary number they cannot act on.

Keep these:

- Bad news, and anything that changed.
- The words still testing it, when it is still being tested.
- A date or a number they have to act on.

Check these:

- Is every line true today?
- Is every number and name right?
- Do you really need the thing you are asking for?

Then read it once on your phone. That is how they will read it.

## The weekly report

It is the same message. Add two or three numbers in one short paragraph, right after the opener. Say which days they cover. Pick the numbers the client makes a decision on.
