# Example updates

Everything on this page is made up. The clients, the people, the work and the numbers are invented to show the shape.

## Example 1: a daily update

Nora runs a small bakery. You built her online order form.

**The facts sheet**

```
Client: Nora. Covers: Tuesday.
Call with her today: no

ALREADY SAID (she knows this, leave it out)
- The new photos are on the menu page (her channel, Monday)

FACTS
- [board, finished] People can pick a pickup time when they order.
- [code, shipped] Sold out items show as sold out and cannot go in the basket.
- [board, finished] Checked her question. People can already order up to 14 days ahead.
- [board, in progress] Reminder text the evening before pickup. It sends. Still being tested, off for real customers.
- [call, Monday] Nora said she would send the holiday opening hours.

THEY ASKED (questions from the client with no answer yet)
- Can people order more than a week ahead? (her channel, Tuesday) Yes, up to 14 days. See the fact above.

WAITING ON THEM
- Holiday opening hours. Blocks the holiday pickup times.

HELD BACK (real work the client does not need to read)
- Moved the form to a new host.
- Fixed two layout bugs on small phones that she never saw.
```

**The update**

```
Hey Nora, quick one.

People can now pick a pickup time when they order. Sold out items show as sold out and can't go in the basket. And yes, people can already order up to 14 days ahead.

I'm working on the reminder text that goes out the evening before pickup. It sends, but I'm still testing it, so it is off for real customers.

One thing I need from you. Can you send me the holiday opening hours you mentioned on Monday? I can't set the holiday pickup times until I have them.
```

**What happened here**

- The photos were left out. She already knew.
- The new host and the layout bugs were left out. She does not need them.
- The reminder text says it is still being tested, because it is.
- Her question from the channel got its answer.
- The ask is last, and it says what it blocks.

## Example 2: two teammates, one update

Omar runs a gym. Maya builds his website. Theo runs his ads. Both worked for him on the same day.

Maya ran `/update Omar` first. Her draft was saved:

```
Hey Omar, quick one.

The class booking page is live. People can book a trial class and they get a confirmation email right away.

Next I'm adding the waitlist for full classes.
```

Theo ran `/update Omar` an hour later. His facts:

```
FACTS
- [board, finished] The trial class ad now points at the new booking page.
- [accounts, this week] The new ad started on Wednesday. Too early to read the numbers.

WAITING ON THEM
- 
```

The skill found Maya's draft and merged the two. Omar gets one message:

```
Hey Omar, quick one.

The class booking page is live. People can book a trial class and they get a confirmation email right away. The trial class ad now points at that page.

Next we're adding the waitlist for full classes. The new ad started on Wednesday, so it is too early to read the numbers.
```

**What happened here**

- Maya's sentences stayed as she wrote them.
- Theo's two facts went where they fit.
- Maya wrote I. Two people did the work, so the merged update says we.
- Maya's first draft was kept as a copy, so nothing she wrote is lost.
- There is no ask, because nobody is waiting on Omar.
